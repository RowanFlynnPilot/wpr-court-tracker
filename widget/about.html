How the Court Tracker works

Where the information comes from, what the newsroom decides, and the guardrails written into the software — in plain English.

What this is

The Marathon County Court Tracker is a hand-picked list of court cases of public interest, followed by the Wausau Pilot & Review newsroom. Each case file pairs what our reporters know with an automatic check of the official court record, so readers can see when a case moves — and what it means — without navigating the court system themselves.

It lives inside stories on wausaupilotandreview.com and as a standalone page. Every case file has its own shareable link.

Where the information comes from

Wisconsin Circuit Court Access (WCCA) is the state court system’s public case-lookup site. For each case, WCCA publishes an official feed that signals when that case’s record was last updated. The court system offers that feed as its sanctioned option for automated access, and it is the only automated source this tracker uses.

The feed tells us that a record changed and when. It does not carry the docket text, the charges, or the hearing schedule — those are still read and written up by people. We never scrape the WCCA website, and the tracker is not the official court record: the Clerk of Circuit Court in the county where a case was filed is the custodian of that record, and every case file links to it.

What the newsroom decides

Editors choose which cases to follow: matters involving public officials, public safety, government, and open records, among others. The list is curated by hand and reviewed by the publisher before anything appears.

Some cases are never tracked, whatever their news value: family, juvenile, guardianship, and mental-health cases. That is editorial policy, and it is written into the software itself — the pipeline recognizes a case’s type from its official number and will not publish one outside the allowed types.

Fairness

Presumption of innocence. Every criminal case carries the note that a charge is an accusation and that every defendant is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty. The software adds it automatically; it cannot be left off.

How cases end stays on the record. Resolved cases move to a “Closed files” drawer rather than disappearing, so readers can see outcomes, not only accusations.

Corrections. If something in a case file is wrong, email news@wausaupilotandreview.com and we will correct the file.

Machines observe; reporters explain

Open any case file and you will see an activity ledger with two kinds of entries:

  • Red: the official court record changed on this date. The entry links to the docket on WCCA. The software wrote it.
  • Teal: what happened, in plain English. A reporter wrote it.

Hearing dates and case summaries are editorial work, typed by reporters from the record. The software keeps the newsroom honest about that division of labor: when a hearing date passes, or the record moves, and no reporter’s note follows, it flags the file for follow-up until one does.

How a case gets added

Reporters use a newsroom entry desk that reads a copy of the case page they saved themselves, prefills a form, and checks the request against editorial policy on the spot. Submissions the publisher signs off on publish at twice-daily sweeps. Nothing is pasted into the tracker by hand, and no part of the process touches the court website automatically.

Readers can ask us to track a case, or ask for help navigating court records, through the “Ask the newsroom” form at the bottom of the tracker.

Reliability and transparency

The record is checked every two hours on weekdays, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Central. When the court system is slow to respond — which it sometimes is for automated checks — the check retries on a fresh machine and the tracker keeps serving its last good data, with the “checked” time shown honestly on the page.

The software, the watchlist, and every change to the data are public on GitHub, released under the MIT license. Anyone can audit what the tracker does and how it decides.

What comes next

Today the tracker knows that a case moved; reporters supply what moved. Subscription access to the court system’s data service — which the newsroom is seeking grant support for — would add official docket entries, charges, and hearing dates automatically. The watchlist, the editorial rules, and the presumption note would stay exactly as they are; only the data layer changes.

Follow along
