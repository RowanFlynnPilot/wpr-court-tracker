What a reader gets: every red entry says what happened, in the court’s own words. Charges and hearing dates come straight from the record. Reporters spend their time on the teal entries: the reporting.

What would not change

  • Editors still choose the cases. The watchlist stays hand-picked and publisher-approved.
  • The rules stay in the software. Family, juvenile, guardianship, and mental-health cases remain blocked, and every criminal case keeps the presumption-of-innocence note.
  • Reporters still explain. Official entries say what the court did; the newsroom says what it means.
  • Only sanctioned access. The data would come from the court system’s own subscription service, never from scraping its website.
